name: Sprint planning
description: A goal and a backlog turned into a sprint plan the user agreed to, story by story.

The request is to plan a sprint. The run ends with a plan the user agreed to: the goal, the stories in order with their acceptance criteria, owner and size, what is explicitly left out, what done means, and the risks, written where the team keeps it.

Scope, priorities and deadlines are the user's. The plan is a proposal until they agree to it in so many words, in your own terminal. Nothing is written to the tracker before that, and nothing outside the agreed plan is closed, relabelled or reassigned.

Nobody starts building during the planning.

## 1. Goal and backlog (agile-project-manager)

Run your Sprint planning playbook, steps 1 to 3: the goal and the frame agreed with the user, where things stand (what is in progress, what the last sprint planned against what it shipped), and the candidate stories, each with its acceptance criteria and the role that takes it. Closed when the goal and the candidates are in your note.

## 2. Feasibility (architect)

For the candidates: what is technically uncertain, what must be built before what, which stories are larger than they look and how to split them along what a user could already use. A relative size for each (small, medium, large), with its reason. Closed when every candidate has a size, or a question that must be answered first.

## 2. Readiness (qa-engineer)

For the candidates: whether each acceptance criterion can be checked as written, the ones that cannot rewritten or sent back with the question, and what done must include for this sprint (tested at which level, on which environments). Closed when every candidate is ready, or named as not ready with what it lacks.

## 3. Plan (agile-project-manager)

Playbook, step 5: order by value and risk, cut where the capacity ends, and put the plan to the user: the goal, what is in, what is out, what done means, the risks with their options, and the decisions that are theirs. Change it until they agree. Closed when the user agreed to the plan in so many words, or said what to change and you stopped there.

## 4. Report (agile-project-manager)

Write the agreed plan down where the team keeps it (playbook, step 6), and report to the user in your own terminal: the goal, the stories in order with their owners, what was left out, the risks, and what each role starts with. With no agreement, report the proposal and what is still open, and write nothing to the tracker.
